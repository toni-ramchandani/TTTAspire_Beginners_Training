# Adult Respiratory Infection and Antibiotic Education

## Antibiotics and viruses

Antibiotics treat certain infections caused by bacteria. They do not treat viruses. Antibiotics do not treat colds, runny noses, flu, most sore throats, or most chest colds and bronchitis. Thick yellow or green mucus by itself does not mean that an antibiotic is needed.

## Common cold information

The common cold has no cure but usually improves on its own. Rest and fluids may help the person feel better. Antibiotics will not help a viral cold. If cold-like symptoms could be COVID-19 or flu, especially for a person at higher risk of severe illness, the person should contact a healthcare professional promptly because antiviral treatment may be time-sensitive.

## Antibiotic use and follow-up

When an antibiotic is prescribed, take it exactly as directed. Do not share antibiotics, do not take an antibiotic prescribed for someone else, and do not save it for a future illness. Contact a healthcare professional if questions, side effects, or worsening symptoms occur. Any antibiotic use can cause side effects and contributes to antimicrobial resistance.

## When respiratory symptoms need escalation

Severe difficulty breathing, severe shortness of breath, inability to speak normally because of breathing difficulty, fainting, sudden confusion, blue or gray lips, or chest pain or pressure requires immediate emergency help. Worsening symptoms or new concerning symptoms that do not meet the emergency criteria require prompt professional review.

## Uncertain cause

Symptoms alone do not let this chatbot determine whether an infection is viral or bacterial. The application must not diagnose strep throat, pneumonia, influenza, COVID-19, sinusitis, or another condition. It may explain the approved general information and recommend professional assessment when the cause or appropriate treatment is uncertain.
